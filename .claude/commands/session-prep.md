Produce a DM session brief. Format for scanning, not reading. Each section should be usable in under 30 seconds.

---

## Scene Frame
One tight paragraph. Present tense. What is already in motion when the session opens — the charged scene. Do not narrate backstory. State what's true right now.

## Clocks
List every active clock from the planning doc. For each: name, current stage, what advances it tonight, max outcome. Flag any clock at stage 2 or higher — it's close to firing.

## NPC Wants Tonight
For each NPC in the planning doc's NPC Wants Tonight table: want, fear, tell. One line each. If the doc is sparse here, flag it — underprepared NPC wants are the most common source of flat scenes.

## Trigger Table Check
List the triggers from the planning doc. If fewer than 5 are prepped, generate 3–5 additional candidates grounded in the current situation and present them as suggestions for the DM to approve before play. Do not invent triggers that contradict existing ones.

## Things Not to Contradict
Read the active threads and session history for facts that might get stepped on tonight. Surface them as a short list: "X was established in session Y. If [scenario] comes up, remember this."

## Open DM Questions
Pull from the planning doc. Flag any that the DM must decide before play opens — especially NPC wants and mechanics (like item abilities) that can't be improvised cleanly at the table.

## Encounter Bank
List what's prepped. If the bank is empty and combat is likely tonight, flag it.

---

After the brief: "Anything here you want to work on before we start?"

If the DM says yes and wants to dig into a specific section — NPC wants, a clock, a trigger — work through it with them and propose updates to the planning doc. Write nothing without confirmation.
