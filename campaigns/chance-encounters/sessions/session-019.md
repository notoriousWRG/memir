The party broke camp at first light inside Casus's fading dome and moved inland with Ollo — navigator's charts out, eager, out of his depth. The fog pulled back from the path ahead as though the island had been waiting. Reading Ollo's maps as they walked, Coriac noticed a prior route marked partway in, different ink, handwriting going smaller and faster before it stopped. Ollo never made it to the Spar Field. He saw it for the first time alongside the rest of them.

The standing spars produced a low chord — deliberate, patterned, a measuring instrument rather than decoration. Grimvald read it on a 20: the field was enchantment grown old, tuned to assess the party's posture toward the island. Shah pulled a spar free before anyone understood what they were standing in, and a corresponding note dropped from the chord. The field was now unbalanced.

The Threshold Warden stood at the far end of the Spar Field holding a whalebone scale — one pan heaped, one empty. It would not open the path. Casus asked it politely — "Do you mind if we pass?" — and then tried to walk around it. The Warden threw him: one massive whalebone arm, Dex save DC 13, Casus rolled 18 and took half — 10 damage, landing back behind Coriac. Coriac healed him. Casus Misty Stepped behind the Warden and found a counterweight recess between its shoulder blades. He tried Suggestion next, but an Arcana 13 told him what he needed to know: construct, immune to charm. He stood down before casting. The party tried the treeline; the fog thickened fast and swallowed sound within steps. Grimvald went in on a survival 21, heard the party, chose to come back. In the end, Gareth read the puzzle and solved it — collected discarded weapons and gear from the ground, loaded the empty pan, got the scale close. Casus placed a cursed sword from an earlier arc on the scales to bring it level.

The Warden stepped aside. Coriac thanked it. It nodded back.

The cursed sword stayed on the scales as they passed.

Moving deeper, the forest went eerily quiet — no surf, no birds, only Ollo writing in his log as he walked. Artcoth caught a figure at the treeline: a silhouette that tracked them as they moved, held his eye, then wasn't there. Not movement. More like it was never solid, and he heard nothing move. Casus's Arcana 24 confirmed it: a projection, a scrying channel, the caster nearby. The whole interior hummed at the register of old divine presence — not constructed, grown. He also clocked the fog: not weather, divine saturation, directional, reactive — it had thinned ahead of them and thickened behind Grimvald the moment he deviated. The island was ushering them in. Coriac's Religion 21 felt Ariel before he named her. Not the storm — the presence beneath the storm, woven into the ground, not hostile to him. The Forge Shard warmed.

The Hollowed Hull rose from the treeline before they registered it — a ship's skeleton half-consumed by the forest, listing in the moss, old enough that wood and root had become the same thing. Smoke from a crooked chimney. Someone singing badly inside, gloriously. Coriac recognized both the song and the voice: Tjorvi, the crew's record-keeper and sometime bard, far into his cups. He sang along from outside.

The song stopped mid-phrase.

Tjorvi came to the hatch — gaunt, one eye swollen nearly shut, still visibly drunk. When Coriac mentioned the bottle, Tjorvi's face flashed dread — Coriac caught it on an insight 18 — before the smile snapped back. He deflected into invitation: "Do come in. The court will come to order." Inside: Onund on a dais of stacked crates, dressed in layered clothes worn like regalia, spreading his hands for arriving petitioners. A rope coiled in neat circles around the dais. A sailcloth partition at the back of the hull, breathing in a draft that had no business being there.

Onund recognized Coriac and willed himself back into the performance. Casus bowed deep and called him "your majesty" — Onund was delighted. Casus asked about the rope; Onund was about to explain it himself when Tjorvi cut him off: "Tradition. Every court needs ceremony." Onund agreed, and the subject closed. While the room held, Artcoth moved through the space on investigation 15 — no one else on this side of the sail, draft coming from through the partition rather than the doors, and the crates under Onund had the wrong weight for empty. At the hoard chest: junk, things that looked nice once, but among them a small length of cord with a knotted sigil that had held up better than everything around it. He pocketed it.

Then Casus pulled out his adventuring rope and made it glow faint blue with prestidigitation, coiling it slowly in his hand. Onund's hand shot out and grabbed Tjorvi's chest. His mouth moved. "The court recognizes this. What is it?" Tjorvi stared alongside him and didn't answer. Casus turned to the smoldering fire and raised a bird from the embers with Control Flames — a small firebird that came out into the room before dissipating. He said "tradition." Onund, in something close to awe, sat back down on his dais with the full weight of someone who had been waiting a long time for something worth looking at. "My wizard. You have blessed us with these gifts. You may ask what you came to ask."

Casus knelt and called Coriac forward. Coriac presented the mission: lost souls, a captain, a crew. Onund received it formally, then the phrase *lost souls* landed and the court dropped out of his voice completely. After a long quiet, and a glance at the sailcloth: "The court has no knowledge of the interior. The court has catalogued what remains." He asked what they offered for the court's knowledge.

Coriac set down the bottle. The one Tjorvi had enchanted and sent out.

Onund didn't register it. Tjorvi made a sound — not a gasp, the opposite — and stared. Onund looked at Tjorvi, then the bottle, then stood up: "You—" Tjorvi said nothing. Onund stormed to the far end of the hull, muttering. He'd had no idea Tjorvi sent it.

Tjorvi sat down on the floor — not a crate, just the floor — and held out his hand. Coriac gave him the bottle. For a moment the drink fell away and there was just a man who had put something out into the ocean not knowing if it would reach anyone. After a long moment: "You came."

Artcoth: "Of course we did." Coriac: "When a bottle shows up in Ravencrest with my name on it, I've got my duty to follow it to my crew. You call, we answer." Coriac cast Cure Wounds on Tjorvi — the infected eye cleared, the fog of drink thinned. One tear. Tjorvi needed a minute and stepped away. He took the bottle back with him. Onund, watching from across the hull, went quiet. For the first time in who knew how long, neither of them was performing anything.

Tjorvi came back and told them what he knew: the captain is inland, deeper. They were separated in the wreck, thought they saw him, tried to follow. He doesn't know if what they saw was truly the captain. The ones in the back went looking and took things they weren't meant to take. "Don't go in there." He looked at Artcoth for a fraction of a second, then away.

Casus and Artcoth went anyway, Gareth with them. Tjorvi watched from the entrance and didn't stop them — just shook his head and said "please don't." Ollo followed Artcoth in and has not picked up his pen since.

Four figures in the cold back room: former crew members frozen mid-reach, mid-sort, mid-open. Grey-blue skin. Barely breathing. Everything they'd taken was gone — the island had collected. One figure's hand extended toward where the thing had been, still reaching. Artcoth's investigation 17 read them each: one counting coins from an empty purse, one holding open a deteriorated box, one with grooves pressed into his palm from something no longer there. Each one looting; each one taken mid-act. The reaching hand went last. He watched the others and reached anyway. Casus's Arcana 12 got the shape: same divine saturation, but concentrated, charged — the presence was holding them, not killing them. He couldn't tell if they were aware.

Artcoth showed Tjorvi the cord from the hoard. Tjorvi held it, confirmed: "This is from our ship." He paused, then handed it back. "Just to be safe — this is a gift."

Tjorvi said they named them all. Wrote the names in the hull wall. Casus knows naming is a rudimentary curse-break. It didn't help.

The party took a short rest in the Hollowed Hull. The session ended there, the sailcloth partition at their backs, the interior ahead.
