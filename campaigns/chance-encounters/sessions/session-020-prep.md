# Session 20 — The Reckoning

---

## 1 — The Charge

The short rest in the Hollowed Hull is over. Two clocks fired overnight: Elara and Wrenn have stopped waiting and started searching — gathering whoever remains, readying the White Gull, watching the interior with nothing to do but wait. The captain is not missing. He walked to the Circled Zone on purpose. He's been there since the grounding, doing his accounting, waiting for the storm to take him on his terms.

*If they do nothing:* Edrin sets the terms of the Reckoning without opposition. The captain finishes alone. Ariel stays bound. Elara takes the helm of a ship going nowhere.

## 5 — Scenes

### Scene 1 — The Hollowed Hull
*[[ariels-island]] — Hollowed Hull sub-location*

**Read-aloud:** "The fire has cooled to ash. The low sound — wind through the keel above, almost not wind — is still there. Through the cut door: the interior. The path you haven't taken. Onund and Tjorvi are where you left them."

**Loaded:** Tjorvi wants the promise honored. Onund wants a dignified goodbye — to be told plainly, not managed. The cord-with-sigil is in Artcoth's kit. The Stilled are in the back room.

**Dig in:**
- Onund quietly asks if they'll bring the crew in the back room along. He knows. He's asking to be told plainly.
- The cord: see [[knotted-sigil-cord]]. Tjorvi gifted it *"just to be safe."* The ward held when everything else in the hoard rotted.
- The interior path through the door: visible, quiet, no fog. The trees at the treeline lean toward the island's center.

**Triggers:**
- *Party tells Onund plainly the Stilled can't be helped* → "Then we stay with them. It's the only thing left." No performance. He means it. Tjorvi: "Someone should know them until the end." They stay.
- *Party tries to bring Onund/Tjorvi against their will* → Tjorvi: "We're not leaving them alone in there." Final.

**Leads to:** Scene 2

### Scene 2 — The Interior
*[[ariels-island]] — The Spar Field and interior path*

**Read-aloud:** "The Spar Field gives way to denser growth. The tones from the standing spars are behind you, replaced by something else — not silence, but the absence of surf. The island has swallowed the sound of the sea. Fog sits at shin height, consistent as water. The moss grows all in one direction."

**Loaded:** Edrin's scry-shadow fires its last tick here — clock 2/3 advancing to 3/3. The image is closer than before, holds eye contact longer, and does not immediately withdraw. He knows they're coming. He's been watching since before they left the hull.

**Dig in:**
- The moss points toward the Circled Zone without exception.
- Ollo marks his map — the circle he drew is exactly ahead of them. "This is it. I said don't go here yet." He keeps walking.
- From this point on, the fog doesn't re-enter the interior. The air smells of ozone.
- DC 13 Perception: the scry-shadow holds long enough to mouth one word before dissolving. Coriac's name.

**Triggers:**
- *Party chases or follows the shadow* → it withdraws; reappears from a different angle, closer
- *Someone addresses Edrin directly* → it stops. Looks. Says: *"He came willingly."* About the captain. Then it's gone.

**Leads to:** Scene 3

### Scene 3 — The Circled Zone: The Captain
*[[ariels-island]] — The Circled Zone*

**Read-aloud (arriving):** "The trees end. The clearing is warmer than the rest of the island by several degrees — charged, like the air before a strike. At its center: a stone frame, twelve feet high, the grey of storm clouds. Inside it, not quite a person, but the shape of one. Wind built around attention, and that attention has already turned toward you. Your ears pop. The fog pushes outward from behind — the threshold without a wall. And at the edge of the clearing, not near the frame, at the place where the trees give way: your captain is standing."

**Loaded:** He walked here on purpose. He's been here since the grounding. He has already done his accounting. The island didn't take him — he came to give himself to it. Ariel is in the frame and knows Coriac is in the room. Edrin is near; not yet visible; waiting to see how this plays.

**Dig in:**
- The captain looks like himself. He's just finished something. First thing he says, to no one specific: *"Did you know?"* He means the Suggestion.
- The stone frame responds to Coriac: not an attack, an attention. The Forge Shard ([[forge-shard]]) pulses and hums. It's warm enough to feel through clothing.
- Ollo goes still. He marks the circle on his map. He puts the pen in his breast pocket and does not take it out again.
- DC 15 Insight: the captain is not afraid. He made his choice before the party arrived.

---

**The captain's scene — play this out:**

He knows what was done. He's been alone with it since the grounding. He's asking who does.

*"The sea doesn't do that. I've known the sea my whole life. It doesn't push a man past his own word."*

He's not hostile. He's accounting.

- If **Grimvald confesses the Suggestion** → he holds it without eruption. He was right. *"I told you what I needed and you went around it."* He doesn't forgive it here. He doesn't need to. He says what he needs to say to whoever was square with him and closes the chapter.
- If **no one confesses** → *"Then I went willingly. That's the story, I suppose."* He doesn't believe it. He accepts it. The cost of silence is that it lives with the table.

To Artcoth, when the time is right: acknowledgment — quiet, direct. "Venn....?"

"That's good. She'll make a good captain."

---

**Read-aloud (when the goodbye is done — the captain calls for the storm):**

"He doesn't announce it. He turns toward the stone frame and doesn't quite reach out — his hand stops short of touching it, and it doesn't need to. The wind answers him. Not wind from outside. Wind from inside the frame, from whatever she is in there, and it comes to him the way a tide comes: patient, certain, without hurry.

He looks back once. At the ship's name, or whatever stands for it in this clearing. Then the wind takes him. Gently. The way the sea takes someone who has walked in to meet it, arms open, eyes open.

The clearing goes still.

Then Edrin Vael steps out from the trees."

---

**What just happened:** Ariel moved of her own will. She received someone who called for her. She chose the manner of it. Edrin's thesis — that she cannot act without being controlled, that ungoverned power only destroys — is already cracked before he opens his mouth.

*Edrin knows this. He steps forward anyway. He has to.*

**Triggers:**
- *Party tries to stop the captain* → he's past stopping. "Don't." Said quietly. Not cruelly. He has earned this choice.
- *Coriac tries to intervene with Ariel* → the wind pauses — a heartbeat — then continues. She heard him. She chose this anyway. It is not the same as not hearing.
- *Edrin steps forward* → **Scene 4**

**Leads to:** Scene 4

### Scene 4 — The Reckoning
*[[ariels-island]] — The Circled Zone | CLIMAX*

**Read-aloud (Edrin steps forward):** "He is nothing like the scry-shadow suggested. Not a ghost — a man who has decided. He looks at the party as obstacles, and then past them to Coriac, and his expression changes: not anger. Not contempt. Recognition. 'You came. I wondered if you had the faith for it.'"

**Loaded:** Edrin is not here to win an argument. He's here to be absolved. He has been alone with this justification since the grounding — no peers, no one capable of understanding what he did or why. Coriac is the first. He wants Coriac specifically to tell him he was right: that binding Ariel was protection, not cruelty, and that anyone who understood the storm's power would have done the same.

*The opening is yours — tie in the old lore here. Let him make the approach before the argument.*

**After the opening — Edrin's case:**

Once the old-lore thread is closed, he turns to the reason he came.

He recounts Halvard — not as a cautionary tale, but with grief. Halvard was brilliant. Probably someone Edrin admired. He'd found a way to commune with Ariel, believed it was a conversation between equals. He was wrong. When she moved through him it wasn't malice — she didn't know what she was doing. That's what ungoverned power does. It doesn't intend harm. It simply causes it.

*"I read what he wrote before the end. His own notes. He knew it was going wrong and he kept reaching. I did what his notes said someone should have done before it reached that point."*

The bind, in Edrin's telling, is the only thing that worked. It kept her from doing it again. To someone else. Possibly to Coriac.

Then the ask — the thing he actually came for:

*"You carry the name. You know what the storm does to those who can't hold it. Tell me Halvard had to die before someone acted. Tell me I was wrong to do the one thing that stopped it."*

He wants Coriac to say: yes. You were right. Protection is love. Control is care.

**The crack:**

Coriac's presence is already the counter-argument — he reached Ariel without binding her and she came willingly. Edrin felt it through the bind the moment it happened on the crossing. He told himself it was the connection finding a channel.

When the voluntary contact is named — by Coriac, by someone else, or by Edrin feeling it land in the silence:

*"She reached you. Voluntarily."*

Not an accusation. A man watching a proof collapse.

*"I felt it through the bind when it happened. I told myself it was the connection finding a channel. That's what I told myself."*

He arrives at this himself if the party engages rather than attacks. If they only fight him, he defends and never gets there. The Reckoning that matters is the one where he hears it — and came in wanting to be told the opposite.

---

**The bind activates.** Ariel attacks. She can't stop it. The stone frame shudders. The wind becomes something with edges. She is present in the clearing in a form too large for the space — and she knows what she's doing. So does anyone watching.

---

> ### HOMEBREW — Ariel's Bind (Freedom Clock, 4 stages)
>
> *Read this aloud before initiative if the bind activates, so the table knows the win condition.*
>
> Ariel acts each round under compulsion. The clock advances when the party hits a trigger. At **4/4**, the bind breaks: Ariel stops, the frame shatters, Edrin loses his hold.
>
> **Clock triggers** — one per trigger, in any order:
> 1. Someone names what Edrin is doing — control dressed as protection — and makes him *hear* it
> 2. Coriac actively reaches toward Ariel (engages, doesn't command — stands in the storm rather than owning it or fleeing it)
> 3. Edrin's justification is cracked — he is confronted, in terms he can't deflect, with the fact that Ariel chose Coriac *voluntarily*
> 4. Coriac stands in it fully — the echo of the lightning strike; he reaches and does not flinch; she finishes the sentence
>
> **Attacking Edrin directly:** Ariel's next action targets the attacker with both available attacks. Attacking him tightens the bind. It doesn't help.
>
> **Ariel's compelled actions each round** (DM's choice per round):
> - *Stormstrike* — +7 to hit; 3d8 lightning to one target
> - *Surge* — 15-ft radius; DC 14 Con save or 2d8 lightning + pushed 10 ft; half on save
> - *Overflow* — DC 13 Wis save or Frightened until end of next turn (she is not aiming this)
>
> *Stat floor: Air Elemental (MM p.124), all damage converted to lightning.*

---

**Dig in:**
- The stone frame is cracking from the inside. Each clock tick, one more crack.
- Ollo takes notes in the margins of his map. He doesn't stop even when the Surge catches him.
- The air smells exactly like the crossing storm. Not a coincidence. The Forge Shard is pulsing steadily.
- The Forge Shard resolves tonight. When Coriac does the thing that defines his relationship to Ariel — speaks for her, refuses to bind her, mediates at real cost to himself — it completes. What it becomes should reflect the specific act. Hold this until it's earned, then decide.

**Edrin if talked down / if the clock fills before he drops:**

He hears it. He cannot maintain the argument. He goes.

*"Tell the crew. Tell them their captain is dead. It's better that they think this."*

He walks into the interior. He doesn't come back. That is exile in the Norse idiom: a man who has no people left to return to.

**Triggers:**
- *Clock reaches 4/4* → Ariel breaks free; read Scene 5 aloud
- *Edrin drops in combat* → bind snaps violently; Ariel surges free uncontrolled for 1 round (Surge, all creatures, no save); then she stills
- *Clock empties (session ends without resolution)* → Edrin maintains control; hard handoff to next session

**Leads to:** Scene 5

### Scene 5 — After
*[[ariels-island]] — Circled Zone → the path back → the beach*

**Read-aloud (bind breaks, 4/4):** "The stone frame shatters from the inside. Not an explosion — a release, the way a fist opens. The wind drops to almost nothing. In that nothing: a voice that has not spoken aloud before. Unhurried. Certain. A little wry.

'The storm follows you.'

She's saying it to Coriac. She has been trying to say it since the crossing."

---

**What she means** — played in layers as Coriac stays open:
1. *Literal, almost wry:* chaos and trouble have followed this crew since the White Gull left harbor. The weather has been hunting them.
2. *Divine:* he carries Thor's name well, and the god comes with it. The storm follows because part of one already walks with him.
3. *Personal:* she reached *him*, of everyone on this island, because he engages the storm without trying to own it. He channels; he does not command. That is the opposite of Edrin. It is why she chose him.

If Coriac asks for guidance: *"Do not bind it. Do not flee it. Stand in it."* — which is also her own plea about her captivity, said sideways.

---

**The bond:** The Reckoning's likely outcome is a willing anchor between Coriac and Ariel — relationship without domination, terms both parties chose. This is not fleshed out tonight. See Section 9.

---

**The Stilled:**

At the moment the bind breaks — back in the Hollowed Hull — time catches up with the four figures in the back room. Edrin's hold on the island was what sustained them. It's gone. They pass. Quietly. Without violence.

Onund and Tjorvi are with them.

When the party returns through the Spar Field, Tjorvi meets them at the Hollowed Hull door. He doesn't say much. *"They're at rest."* That's the report. That's the arc closed.

---

**The beach:**

Elara Venn is not on the ship. She's on the black sand with whoever she could gather, watching the interior. She stopped moving when the island changed.

She reads their faces. She reads Ollo's face — she knows Ollo.

*"He's not coming back."* Not a question.

If the captain's words reach her — *"Elara will know what to do"* — she holds it. A long moment. *"I know."* She has been carrying the weight since the grounding. This just makes it official.

**Elara Venn is the new captain of the White Gull.**

That is the fact the world ends session 20 with.

## 9 — Decide Before Play

🔴 **Must resolve before the session opens:**

1. **The Forge Shard resolution** — the item file says it completes through a roleplay act, and "what it becomes should reflect the specific act." The Reckoning is that act. Decide what Coriac's specific defining moment looks like and what the shard becomes. *Options: it becomes a focus for storm-sense (he can feel weather as Ariel feels it); it becomes the physical token of the bond (the anchor between them); it cracks and the power is absorbed into Coriac's body rather than held separately.* Don't announce it at the table until the act happens.

2. **Edrin's ending refinement** — if he's fought and dropped, the bind snaps violently. If he's talked down: *"Tell the crew. Their captain is dead. It's better that they think this."* He goes into exile. Decide if you want a third option (he dissolves into the island — he was holding on through the bind and without it he has nothing).

⚠️ **Would benefit from a decision before play:**

3. **The Ariel/Coriac bond — future mechanic** — the Reckoning establishes a willing anchor between Coriac and Ariel. The bond needs mechanical design before it's used at the table. Thematic direction:
   - Grows through acts aligned with *"stand in it, don't bind it, don't flee it"*
   - Passive (early): storm-sense — he feels weather coming the way Ariel feels it; can send/receive across stormy seas
   - Active (later): communion — he can ask Ariel something and she can answer, once per storm
   - Deep (arc close): shared will in storms — he can call weather that she shapes, or vice versa
   - She can *ask* things of him (not commands); he can refuse; refusing costs the bond
   - This is a relationship, not a patron. Design it to play like one.
   - *Build this out before Session 21 if the bond lands tonight.*

4. **What Ollo does now** — he charted the island, witnessed the Reckoning, saw the Stilled pass. His arc has shifted. Is he coming off the island with the party? Does he stay? Does he ask Dessa something he couldn't ask before? Worth knowing before the beach scene plays.
