## Arc 1 — The Frostveil

The party assembled in Ravencrest, pointed toward a job they didn't fully understand. A man named Cole — an operative of an organization called the Circle — hired them to steal a ruby ring from Lady Grimhook's manor, inscribed inside with the name *Liriel, "song of the Night."* A fence named Jimmy Quickhands laid out the approaches. The gala at Lord Thorne's estate became the window.

At the gala, Thorne used a Rod of Rulership to publicly humiliate Grimhook before his guests — made her kneel, made her speak words of admiration she didn't mean. The party moved to take the rod during the chaos. They failed: the animated armor in the gallery put Gareth down and the rod wasn't retrieved. What they'd done without knowing it was clear the gallery long enough for Grimhook's own agent to get in and out. She emerged from the crowd holding the rod. She broke its tip against the floor, told the guests it was a staged bit of theatre announcing a new business partnership, then used the rod's remaining influence to make Thorne confirm the lie. She tousled his hair on the way out.

She hired them afterward. Directly, eyes open — because they'd proven useful precisely by being observed rather than effective. The commission: enchanted bracers belonging to Zal'goroth, a shaman in exile somewhere in the Ironspine Mountains.

The Circle wasn't finished. Jimmy intercepted the party on the Ironspine road with two others — he wanted the ring. Artcoth gave the signal. The fight ended with Jimmy dead at 140 feet, an arrow through him in the tree line. An encrypted note on his body pointed to the Circle's coordinator, Coal, by name: *"Enough excuses. I've been patient enough. Take care of them."* The Circle knew the party hadn't delivered. Jimmy was gone.

Into the mountains. The underdark opened beneath them: bioluminescent fungi, compressed silence, tunnels shaped over centuries and partially abandoned. A war mage held a bridge deeper in — Shah Doh was charmed, turned on Coriac, and then broke it himself. The tunnels opened into a lightless lake. [[shalindra-aboleth]] surfaced.

She called herself the King-slayer. She wore the signet ring of an ancient dwarven monarch on her fang like a trophy. She offered safe passage into the duergar city through her route in exchange for the party clearing an infestation she wanted gone — and she demonstrated her leverage before they could negotiate: a duergar thrall slit his own throat on her command. Then she handed them the signet ring as the key to the underwater passage. The pact was entered.

The duergar city above was the ancient dwarven capital rebuilt: functional spaces enlarged, ornament stripped out, ceilings lowered. [[casus]] joined the party in the library — having arrived separately, by a route that was never fully accounted for. The arc resolved at the Soul Forge: Zal'goroth was defeated. [[ovaltine-jenkins]] played a role in the escape. They surfaced. Grimhook received the bracers and pointed them toward the next commission: the Pendant of Stars.

Whether the pact with Shalindra was fulfilled to her satisfaction was never asked.

## Arc 2 — The Storm That Remembers

Downtime in Ravencrest. Ovaltine's shop produced a rift — small, not sustained, but a threshold crossed, with the party present. Grimhook visited and the Pendant of Stars terms were formalized into a contract with specific stakes. Before the voyage, the party acquired a headquarters: two properties competed — a planar curiosity shop with rooms that didn't connect the same way twice, and a genuinely haunted house that had opinions about who moved through it. The party made their choice.

Coriac received a message in a bottle. An SOS that found him specifically — sent by Tjorvi, the White Gull's record-keeper, from somewhere ahead of them.

The White Gull was acquired through Grimhook's contract. Its captain was not a simple contractor — his relationship to the sea, the crew, and this particular voyage carried texture the party would need to understand. On the crossing, the storm came on deliberately. It intensified in phases — weather that began as bad and became something with intention, something directing them rather than obstructing them. Grimvald had a moment in the early crossing that held the crew together when morale was close to breaking. He cast Suggestion on the captain. The captain pressed on against his own stated condition without knowing why.

Phase 3 of the storm carried the White Gull toward Ariel's island. Coriac stepped into a lightning strike to protect the ship. [[ariel]] pulled him into a vision — a vast female form, crackling eyes, storm built into flesh. He reached out his hand and invited her to speak. She was shocked: no mortal had done that before. She moved toward speech. The vision ended before she could finish. She has not finished what she was about to say.

The White Gull grounded on black sand. The captain was missing — no body.

Items from the storm crossing: [[artcoths-gloves]] from the beach wreckage, [[shield-of-storms-mercy]], [[stormglass-shard]], [[storm-notation]], [[wardens-bone]].

Five [[storm-draugr]] emerged from the fog and the surf.

The fight on the beach was won and it felt like nothing. They had fought like the sailors they'd been — footing, leverage, the waterline as territory. Toward the end, with their numbers thinning, they did something undead aren't supposed to do: coordinated a retreat. One gestured another back toward the water. The one who gave the signal stayed a beat longer to cover the escape. He was struck down where he stood. The ones who got away surfaced offshore and watched. The party walked off the sand carrying a victory nobody wanted.

Inland of the wrecks: a fire, and a crude perimeter of rope and bone. The island's survivors — [[marek]] offering stew before anyone had said why they came, [[ollo]] with notes and theories and questions he'd been too afraid to ask, [[dessa]] giving the party the patterns of the place and none of its truth before she finished her knot and went to bed. What the party learned: a crew had walked inland weeks ago and never came back, and their old captain had been sighted from the treeline — though the survivors debated whether anyone was truly seeing him or just seeing things.

The party chose the interior. [[ollo]] asked to come and they let him. They rested in the dome [[casus]] raised against the fog.

## Arc 2 — Into the Interior

The party broke camp at first light and moved inland. Ollo navigated with his charts, noting that a previous route was marked partway in — different ink, handwriting growing smaller and faster before it stopped. He had never made it to the Spar Field. He saw it for the first time alongside the rest of them.

The standing spars produced a low chord — deliberate, patterned, a measuring instrument grown old. Grimvald read it: the field was enchantment tuned to assess the party's posture toward the island. Shah Doh pulled a spar free before anyone understood what they were standing in. A corresponding note dropped from the chord. The field was now unbalanced.

The Threshold Warden stood at the far end holding a whalebone scale — one pan heaped, one empty. It would not open the path. The party tested its limits: Casus asked politely and then tried to walk around it, and the Warden threw him across the field. Casus Misty Stepped behind it, investigated, stood down when he confirmed it was immune to charm. Grimvald went into the fog; it swallowed sound within steps, and he chose to come back. In the end, Gareth read the puzzle — collected discarded gear from the ground, loaded the empty pan, got the scale close. Casus placed a cursed sword from an earlier arc on the scales to bring it level.

The Warden stepped aside. Coriac thanked it. It nodded back.

The cursed sword stayed on the scales as they passed.

Deeper in, the forest went quiet — no surf, no birds. Artcoth caught a figure at the treeline tracking them, holding his eye, then gone — not movement, more like it was never solid. Casus's arcane read of the interior: a scrying projection with a caster nearby; the fog not weather but divine saturation, directional and reactive — it had thinned ahead of them and thickened behind Grimvald the moment he deviated. The island was ushering them in. Coriac felt Ariel's presence in the ground before he named it — not hostile to him. The [[forge-shard]] warmed.

The Hollowed Hull rose from the treeline: a ship's skeleton half-consumed by the forest, listing in the moss, old enough that wood and root had become the same thing. Smoke from a crooked chimney. Someone singing badly inside, gloriously. Coriac recognized both the song and the voice — Tjorvi, the White Gull's record-keeper, far into his cups. He sang along from outside.

The song stopped mid-phrase.

Tjorvi came to the hatch — gaunt, one eye swollen, still visibly drunk. Inside: Onund on a dais of stacked crates dressed in layered clothes worn like regalia, spreading his hands for arriving petitioners. A sailcloth partition at the back of the hull breathing in a draft that had no business being there. Artcoth moved through the space: no one else on this side of the sail, the draft coming through the partition rather than the doors. In the hoard chest: junk, but among it a small cord with a knotted sigil that had held up better than everything around it. He pocketed it.

Casus made his adventuring rope glow faint blue and coiled it slowly. Onund's hand shot out and grabbed Tjorvi's chest. His mouth moved: *"The court recognizes this."* Casus raised a firebird from the fire with a cantrip — it came out into the room and dissipated. He said: *"Tradition."* Onund sat back down with the weight of someone who had been waiting a long time for something worth looking at. *"My wizard. You have blessed us. You may ask what you came to ask."*

Casus knelt and called Coriac forward. Coriac presented the mission: lost souls, a captain, a crew. Onund received it formally, then the phrase *lost souls* landed and the court dropped out of his voice. After a long quiet, and a glance at the partition: *"The court has no knowledge of the interior. The court has catalogued what remains."* He asked what they offered for the court's knowledge.

Coriac set down the bottle. The one Tjorvi had enchanted and sent out.

Onund didn't register it. Tjorvi made a sound — not a gasp, the opposite — and stared. Onund looked at Tjorvi, then the bottle, then stood: *"You—"* Tjorvi said nothing. Onund stormed to the far end of the hull, muttering. He hadn't known Tjorvi had sent it.

Tjorvi sat down on the floor — not a crate, just the floor — and held out his hand. Coriac gave him the bottle. The drink fell away for a moment and there was just a man who had put something out into the ocean not knowing if it would reach anyone. *"You came."*

Artcoth: *"Of course we did."* Coriac: *"When a bottle shows up in Ravencrest with my name on it, I've got my duty to follow it to my crew. You call, we answer."* He cast Cure Wounds on Tjorvi. The infected eye cleared. One tear. Tjorvi needed a minute and stepped away.

When he came back, he told them what he knew: the captain is inland, deeper. They were separated in the wreck, thought they saw him, tried to follow. He doesn't know if what they saw was truly the captain. The ones who went in the back looking for things to take — they didn't come back. *"Don't go in there."* He looked at Artcoth for a fraction of a second, then away.

Casus, Artcoth, and Gareth went in anyway. Ollo followed and has not picked up his pen since.

Four figures in the cold back room: former crew, frozen mid-reach, mid-sort, mid-open. Grey-blue skin. Barely breathing. Everything they'd taken was gone — the island had collected. One figure's hand extended toward where the thing had been, still reaching. They had each been looting; they had each been taken mid-act. The presence in that room was the same divine saturation as the rest of the island — but concentrated, charged. Holding them, not killing them.

Artcoth showed Tjorvi the cord from the hoard. Tjorvi held it, confirmed: *"This is from our ship."* He paused, then handed it back. *"Just to be safe — this is a gift."*

The names were written in the hull wall. It didn't help.

The party took a short rest in the Hollowed Hull. The sailcloth partition is at their backs. The interior is ahead.
