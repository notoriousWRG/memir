The deities of Ældor span from the creator-gods of Asgard and Vanaheim to the antagonist children of Loki, whose banishment shapes the world's present age.

---

## The Aesir

**Odin** — *Wisdom, War, Magic, Poetry, Death.* Symbol: twin ravens (Huginn and Muninn), an eight-legged horse (Sleipnir). The All-Father, first among the gods of Asgard. He shaped the world from Ymir's body, fashioned the first humans, and built the divine order that the Age of Harmony rested upon. His ravens fly across the realms and return to tell him what they have seen. His followers are warriors, scholars, and seers. It was Odin's prophetic vision of Loki's children ending the world that sparked the divine war that closed the Age of Harmony.

**Thor** — *Thunder, Strength, Protection, Fertility.* Symbol: Mjolnir. Protector of mortals and champion of Asgard, worshipped by warriors seeking strength in battle, farmers who need the rains, and sailors who need the storms to pass. The prophecy of Jormungandr foretells that Thor will slay the World Serpent at the end of days — but will himself fall nine steps later, dead from its venom.

**Baldr** — *Light, Purity, Beauty, Rebirth.* Symbol: a radiant sun. God of light and purity, beloved among the gods and mortals alike. He represents the possibility of goodness persisting in a world that seems designed to extinguish it. His followers — healers, poets, those seeking forgiveness — come to him with hope. The legend of his death and eventual rebirth is one of Ældor's most enduring myths.

**Tyr** — *War, Justice.* Symbol: a spear or a single hand. The divine guarantor that strength serves law rather than mere appetite. His most celebrated act was sacrificing his hand to bind Fenrir — placing it in the wolf's mouth as surety for a promise the gods knew they would break. His followers are warriors who fight within a code, judges who hold to it when they should not, and law-makers who understand that law is only as good as the sacrifice backing it.

**Frigg** — *Marriage, Motherhood, Prophecy.* Symbol: a spinning wheel. Guardian of the household and the bond between partners. She sees the future but rarely speaks of it. Her followers are mothers, wives, and homemakers who find the sacred in the ordinary — the thread pulled tight, the hearth kept, the family held together.

**Bragi** — *Poetry, Music, Eloquence.* Symbol: a harp or a quill. Divine patron of bards and storytellers, keeper of the tales that hold the ages together. Without Bragi's followers, the history of Ældor would exist only as ruins. His followers are bards, poets, and storytellers who understand that memory is a form of power.

**Idunn** — *Youth, Immortality.* Symbol: a golden apple. Keeper of the golden apples that sustain the gods' immortality — without her, the gods age. Her followers seek longevity, healing, and the renewal of what has begun to fail.

**Víðarr** — *Forests, Vengeance.* Symbol: a boot or a wolf. Son of Odin, a god of few words and steady purpose. He dwells in deep forests and rarely acts — but when he does, the act is final. His followers are rangers, avengers, and survivalists who understand that the most dangerous force is one that waits.

## The Vanir

**Frey** — *Fertility, Prosperity, Sunlight.* Symbol: a golden boar. Patron of abundant harvests and good fortune, invoked over fields and markets alike. His followers are farmers seeking good harvests, merchants seeking good trades, and anyone seeking abundance.

**Freyja** — *Love, Beauty, War, Magic.* Symbol: Brísingamen (a necklace), a chariot pulled by cats. A goddess of contradictions held in perfect tension: love and war, beauty and magic. The most widely worshipped of the Vanir among mortals, sought by lovers, warriors, and practitioners of seiðr alike.

**Njord** — *Sea, Prosperity.* Symbol: a ship or waves. Patron of all who make their living on the water — sailors, fishermen, and coastal merchants above all. Ship blessings performed before every departure invoke his name. His followers understand that the sea feeds and kills in equal measure, and they honor Njord because they have no other choice.

**Yavanna** — *Nature, Growth.* Symbol: a flowering tree. Called the Giver of Fruits, Yavanna watches over every seed that becomes a field and every field that feeds a people. A goddess of patience and deep time, revered by druids, farmers, and those who protect growing things.

## Neutral Powers

**Nienna** — *Compassion, Healing.* Symbol: a tear. Called the Lady of Mercy, Nienna witnesses suffering without flinching and teaches that grief is not weakness but the measure of what mattered. Her followers are healers, caregivers, and those seeking solace — anyone who has had to keep going anyway.

**Tilion** — *Moon, Hunting.* Symbol: a silver bow. The Hunter of the Moon, Tilion guides those who move through darkness — navigators reading the night sky, hunters who track by moonlight, travelers who have no other landmark to follow.

## The Antagonists

These powers were banished, bound, or exiled following the divine war that ended the Age of Harmony. They remain active through followers, agents, and influence from their places of exile.

**Loki** — *Chaos, Deception.* Symbol: a twisted serpent or a shattered mask. God of chaos and deception, Loki began as a chaotic ally to Odin before his hidden children and mounting deceptions brought the Age of Harmony to an end. He is now bound in unbreakable chains. Prophecy holds that he will escape, and that when he does, it will not go well for anyone.

**Hel** — *Death, Decay.* Symbol: a half-decayed face or a skeletal hand. Daughter of Loki, ruler of Helheim. Cast into the underworld by Odin, Hel struck a deal rather than accepting punishment: dominion over the realm of the dead in exchange for maintaining order there — keeping the dead in and preventing undead incursions. Her rule is neutral in practice but fierce in defense of her territory.

**Skadi** — *Winter, Vengeance.* Symbol: a snowflake with a dagger. Goddess of winter and vengeance, ruler of the frozen reaches of Niflheim. Her wrath is slow and deliberate — she views emotion as weakness and control as the only legitimate form of power.

**Surtur** — *Fire, Destruction.* Symbol: a flaming sword crossing a broken crown. King of Fire, ruler of Muspelheim. Unlike Skadi, who craves control, Surtur revels in unbridled freedom and encourages his followers to embrace their passions without restraint.

**Váli** — *Discord, Madness.* Symbol: a broken mirror or a fractured mask. Born from Loki's chaotic essence, Váli thrives on discord and madness, spreading fear and nightmares from exile. When someone acts without reason — when betrayal has no motive, violence has no aim — Váli's name is invoked as both explanation and warning.
