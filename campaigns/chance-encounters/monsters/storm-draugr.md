Not mindless undead. They were sailors once. They move like sailors — they understand footing, water, leverage, and the difference between a fair fight and a smart one.

**They do not fall when killed. They dissolve.** Each death: the body unravels, turning to salt-heavy mist that moves back toward the water with something like intention. The beach absorbs them. The fog tightens slightly after each one falls, as if the island is noting the outcome.

## Tide Step

As a bonus action after hitting a target, a Storm Draugr can teleport up to 30 feet to an unoccupied space it can see. Used to maintain flanking positions, reposition after a hit, and keep the party from feeling settled.

## Behavior

**They split focus intentionally.** When a draugr has two targets to choose from, it picks the one already threatened. They're not trying to prove something — they're trying to finish the fight.

**They prefer the water's edge.** Draugr pushed back toward the surf don't retreat — they reset. The water partially restores them cosmetically: wisps of fog bleed off their skin, they straighten. The waterline is their territory.

**They coordinate without signals.** Two draugr near each other will orient simultaneously toward the same target without any visible communication. That coordination is the most unsettling thing about them.

**They do not speak.** But they gesture — an open mouth that produces only the sound of distant water, a slow turn of the head toward a threat the party hasn't noticed yet.

**They cover each other's retreat.** Established in the beach fight: when their numbers thin, they will break off rather than fight to the last. One gestures another back toward the water; the one who gives the signal stays a beat longer to cover the escape, and is the one who falls. It is the most human thing they do, and the hardest to kill against.
